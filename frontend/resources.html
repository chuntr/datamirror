Key Concepts

Understanding the vocabulary of online tracking helps you make better decisions about the tools you use and the permissions you grant.

What Are Cookies?
Cookies are small files stored by websites on your device to remember preferences, login sessions, and browsing behavior. First-party cookies (from the site you're visiting) are mostly harmless — third-party cookies from advertisers are what fuel cross-site tracking.
What Is Browser Fingerprinting?
Browser fingerprinting combines details about your device, browser version, screen resolution, installed fonts, and dozens of other signals to identify you — even without cookies. It's harder to block because it leaves no file on your computer.
What Is a Data Broker?
Data brokers collect, package, and sell personal information gathered from public records, apps, purchases, and online activity. Your name, address, income estimate, and browsing habits may already be for sale without your knowledge.
What Are Tracking Pixels?
Tracking pixels are tiny invisible images or code snippets embedded in emails and webpages. When loaded, they tell the sender your IP address, email client, location estimate, and when you opened the message — all without any visible notification.
What Is End-to-End Encryption?
End-to-end encryption means only the sender and receiver can read a message — not the service provider in between. Apps like Signal use this by default. Standard SMS, email, and many chat apps do not.
What Is a VPN?
A Virtual Private Network routes your internet traffic through a server in another location, hiding your real IP address from websites and your internet provider. It reduces one form of tracking but is not a complete privacy solution on its own.
